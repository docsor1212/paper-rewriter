We retrospectively reviewed 176 patients who underwent laparoscopic cholecystectomy at a single teaching hospital between 2022 and 2025. Operations were performed or supervised by three senior surgeons, and intraoperative recordings were reviewed for conversion events.

Patients were grouped by preoperative gallbladder wall thickness. The primary outcome was conversion to open surgery; secondary outcomes included operative time, hospital stay and readmission within 30 days.

Wall thickness above 5 mm was associated with longer operative times. Two conversions occurred, both in the thick-wall group; neither resulted in bile duct injury.

Statistical analysis used Stata 18. Continuous variables were compared with the Mann-Whitney U test, and categorical variables with Fisher's exact test.

Limitations include the retrospective design and the small number of conversions, which restricts multivariable modelling. We conclude that routine ultrasound remains a useful preoperative marker, and that consent conversations should reflect the elevated conversion risk in thick-wall gallbladders.
